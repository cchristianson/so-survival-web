On foot, by bicycle and by car.

Straight-line distance: 266 miles. ⬇️ Download the GPS track with all the routes (import into OsmAnd, Avenza or Gaia — works with no signal).

Route 1 — Back roads (avoids freeways)

Stays off freeways and uses county, Forest Service and BLM roads and trails: quieter and less exposed, but often longer, steeper, more remote, and snow-prone on high passes. Forest/BLM road numbers match the maps in Maps\Forest-Service.

Route 2 — Highway route (uses freeways)

Follows the main highways, freeways included (US-199, I-5, US-101 and others): usually the shortest distance, gentler grades, real bridges, easy to follow, towns and abandoned vehicles along the way — but exposed, and where other people will be.

Route 3 — By bicycle

A route for bicycles: uses bike paths and quiet roads where it can, and freeways only where bicycles are allowed. See the Bicycles & carts guide for loads, trailers and repairs.

Distance397 miles
Riding timeabout 64 hours of riding with a load at about 6 mph — about 11 days riding 6 hours a day. Add about 1 hour for every 1,000 ft of climbing, plus rests and repairs.

After a disaster, expect closed or damaged roads, checkpoints and no fuel for sale: plan on foot or by bicycle and carry the paper directions.

These routes come from OpenStreetMap data (Nov 2026). They know nothing about washed-out or burned bridges, landslides, snow, fire, river levels, gates, water or food. Plan water every day; check the route on a paper map before you set out.
